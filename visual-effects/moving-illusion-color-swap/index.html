Rotating Snakes

A peripheral drift illusion by Akiyoshi Kitaoka, Professor at Ritsumeikan University, Kyoto. First published in 2003.

The image is entirely static — nothing actually moves. The sensation of rotation is triggered by rapid eye movements (saccades). When your gaze shifts, the asymmetric luminance steps in each color band fool peripheral motion detectors in the visual cortex. Adjacent circles use mirrored color spirals, making them appear to spin in opposite directions.

Try fixing your gaze on a single point — the motion stops.
